# Illustrative pilot impact model

## Observed baseline

- Supplied quarter: 5,000 fictional leads and 368 activations
- Observed lead-to-activation: 368 / 5,000 = 7.36%, displayed as 7.4%
- Proposed pilot target: 10% lead-to-activation

## Scenario calculation

- At the same 5,000-lead volume, 10% implies 500 activations.
- Increment versus observed count: 500 - 368 = 132 additional activations in a comparable quarter.
- Example micro-bundle monthly price: ₹1,797.
- Example one-site professional-office monthly price: ₹3,171.
- If every one of the 132 incremental activations chose the micro example: 132 × ₹1,797 = ₹237,204 (about ₹2.37 lakh gross list-price monthly run-rate).
- If every one chose the single-site clinic example: 132 × ₹3,171 = ₹418,572 (about ₹4.19 lakh gross list-price monthly run-rate).

## What the scenario does not claim

This range is a sensitivity illustration, not a forecast, revenue-recognition estimate, contribution margin, profit or ROI. It assumes the observed lead volume repeats, treats all incremental wins as one example bundle price, and excludes actual product mix, discounts, taxes, setup fees, activation timing, costs, churn, and any change in sales-channel investment.

Churn is absent from the supplied lead file. The case brief's ₹15,000 blended CAC and the approximately ₹5,385 mean `acquisition_cost_inr` among activated CSV rows do not reconcile. A pilot should instrument product mix, booked/activated timing, gross margin, acquisition and servicing costs, and churn before making financial-return claims.
